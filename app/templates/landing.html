01 / THE CHALLENGE

Fire behaves differently
when gravity changes.

In microgravity, buoyancy-driven convection is greatly reduced. Flames, heat and smoke can behave differently from what we observe on Earth.

NASA has conducted valuable combustion investigations aboard the International Space Station and other platforms. But the information is distributed across investigation records, technical reports and datasets. Finding relevant experiments, comparing their conditions and understanding their implications can take significant effort.

The challenge is not simply a lack of data. It is making scientific evidence easier to discover, compare and interpret.

03 / FIRE-SAFETY RESEARCH

Three pillars. One mission.

IGNIS groups investigations into platform-defined research categories. These are IGNIS classifications, not official NASA rankings.

01PREVENTION
◇

Prevent ignition
and flame spread.

Research into material flammability, ignition behaviour and flame spread. Examples include SAFFIRE and BASS.

02DETECTION
◎

Understand smoke
and detection.

Research into smoke particle properties, growth and transport, including SAME and SAME-R.

03SUPPRESSION
⌁

Study flame
extinction.

Research into flame extinction and suppressant behaviour, including FLEX.

SUPPORTING RESEARCH Additional investigations, such as SPICE and flame-spread modelling, provide scientific context without being presented as direct tests of spacecraft fire-safety systems.

05 / TRUST AND TRACEABILITY

Three dimensions.
Never one misleading score.

01

Scientific relevance

Does the investigation address the selected fire-safety problem?

02

Experimental comparability

How closely do the supplied conditions match documented experiments?

03

Evidence availability

What source records, metadata, numerical results or telemetry are integrated?

IGNIS does not present its relevance categories as NASA rankings. A missing local dataset does not mean an investigation lacks scientific relevance. The platform does not claim to predict fire behaviour or certify spacecraft safety.

Before you begin

Online access
The deployed IGNIS application can be explored directly through your browser. Local installation is not required for online access.

Local requirements
Python 3.11 or later, Git, and an internet connection. The clean installation was tested using Python 3.14.7.

Gemini API
Enables AI-assisted scientific interpretation. Obtain an API key through Google AI Studio.

NASA FIRMS
Enables satellite fire and thermal-anomaly observations. Obtain a MAP_KEY through NASA FIRMS.

NASA PSI datasets
Some local-data features depend on additional research files being available in the repository.

Vercel configuration
Configure production API credentials through Vercel Project Settings → Environment Variables. Do not upload your local .env file.

Security
Never publish API keys, private credentials, or environment files.

EXAMPLE ENVIRONMENT CONFIGURATION 
GEMINI_API_KEY=your_gemini_api_key
NASA_FIRMS_MAP_KEY=your_nasa_firms_map_key

Both API integrations are optional. Features depending on unavailable credentials may be limited.
